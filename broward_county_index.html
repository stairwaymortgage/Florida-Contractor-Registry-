About Construction Licensing in Broward County

Broward County is home to 21,531 actively licensed Florida construction professionals — second only to Miami-Dade in the state. The Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) oversees all contractor licensing through the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB), which issues two distinct categories of license under Florida Statute Chapter 489.

Certified contractors hold state-issued licenses (prefixed with "C") granting authority to work in any Florida county. Registered contractors hold local jurisdiction licenses (prefixed with "R") authorizing work within specific counties only. As of the latest DBPR refresh, 19,418 Broward-based contractors hold active, current licenses.

Hiring a Contractor in Broward County

Before signing any contract, Florida homeowners should verify the contractor's license at myfloridalicense.com, confirm the license type matches the scope of work, and request proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage. Performing unlicensed contracting work on projects exceeding $5,000 is a third-degree felony under Fla. Stat. § 489.127.

For projects involving multiple trades — such as a major renovation requiring general contracting, roofing, and plumbing — homeowners may benefit from working with a contractor holding multiple certifications, reducing the need for subcontracting and simplifying contract management.

How many licensed contractors are in Broward County?
As of May 24, 2026, 21,531 contractors are licensed and headquartered in Broward County, of which 19,418 hold an active, current license status. The remainder are voluntarily inactive but maintain current licenses.
What's the difference between a Certified and a Registered contractor in Florida?
Certified contractors (license prefix "C") are licensed by the state and can work anywhere in Florida. Registered contractors (license prefix "R") are licensed through local jurisdictions and can only work in their licensed county or municipality. For projects in Broward, either type is valid, but certified contractors have broader authority.
How do I verify a Broward contractor's license is current?
Every contractor profile on this site links directly to the official DBPR verification page for that license. You can also search by license number or contractor name at myfloridalicense.com. License status can change between our weekly data refreshes, so we recommend confirming directly with DBPR before signing any contract.
What types of work require a licensed contractor in Florida?
Any construction, remodeling, repair, or improvement work on a structure typically requires a licensed contractor. Florida law (Fla. Stat. § 489.127) makes unlicensed contracting a third-degree felony for projects exceeding $5,000. Specific trades — including electrical, plumbing, roofing, mechanical, and HVAC — require additional specialty licenses beyond a general contractor license.
