Licensed vs Unlicensed Contractors in Alachua County

Learn how to tell if a Florida contractor is properly licensed in Alachua County using public records, and why it matters before you hire.

You can tell if a Florida contractor is properly licensed by looking up their license number in the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) public database or through a private directory like Florida Contractor Registry, which compiles the same public records. Check that the license is active, covers the trade you need, and is held in the contractor's actual name or company name.

Why does hiring a licensed contractor matter in Alachua County?

Hiring a licensed contractor protects you legally and financially. In Alachua County, as throughout Florida, building permits often require a licensed contractor to sign off on the work. If unlicensed work is later discovered during a sale, refinance, or insurance claim, you may be the one responsible for correcting it.

Gainesville, the county seat, has an active housing market with older homes that frequently need roof replacements, HVAC upgrades, and structural repairs. These are exactly the project types where licensing requirements are strictly enforced. An unlicensed contractor may be cheaper upfront, but the downstream risk falls on the homeowner.

Public accountability is also a factor. Licensed contractors are held to standards set by the DBPR, can have their licenses suspended or revoked, and are subject to consumer complaints. Unlicensed operators have no such accountability structure.

How can you tell if a Florida contractor is properly licensed?

The most direct way is to ask the contractor for their license number and then confirm it yourself through a public records lookup. Florida contractor licenses are public records under Chapter 119, Florida Statutes, which means anyone can access them.

Here is a step-by-step process:

  1. Ask the contractor for their Florida license number and the name under which it is issued.
  2. Visit a lookup tool such as Florida Contractor Registry or the DBPR's own site to search the license.
  3. Confirm the license status shows as active, not expired, suspended, or revoked.
  4. Confirm the license type matches the work you are hiring for — a roofing license does not authorize electrical work.
  5. Confirm the name on the license matches the person or company you are dealing with.
  6. Check whether the contractor has any open disciplinary history in the public record.

What types of contractor licenses exist in Florida?

Florida issues licenses across many trade categories, and each type covers specific work. Hiring the wrong license type is as problematic as hiring someone with no license at all.

Common Florida contractor license categories include:

  • General Contractor — broad construction, remodeling, and structural work
  • Roofing Contractor — roof installation and repair
  • Electrical Contractor — wiring, panels, and electrical systems
  • Plumbing Contractor — pipes, fixtures, and drainage systems
  • HVAC Contractor — heating, ventilation, and air conditioning
  • Pool/Spa Contractor — pool construction and equipment
  • Specialty Contractor — narrower trades such as solar, insulation, or painting

You can browse license types to understand which applies to your project at the contractor types guide. Matching the license to the job is a step many homeowners skip, but it matters for both safety and insurance purposes.

How does license verification work in Alachua County specifically?

In Alachua County, state-certified contractors can work without additional local approval. State-registered contractors, by contrast, are limited to the jurisdictions where they have registered and may need local approval in Gainesville or other cities within the county.

When you search a license, the public record will indicate whether it is state-certified or state-registered. If a contractor claims they are licensed to work in Alachua County, the record should confirm that scope. If it does not, ask for clarification before signing anything.

Alachua County's building department handles permit issuance locally, and permit records are separate from license records. Both are worth checking. You can explore how permits relate to contractor accountability at the permits guide.

What are the warning signs that a contractor may not be licensed?

Certain patterns commonly appear when a contractor is operating without proper licensing. None of these alone is definitive, but each warrants closer investigation.

  • They cannot or will not provide a license number
  • They ask to be paid entirely in cash with no written contract
  • They offer a price significantly below other bids without explanation
  • They suggest skipping the permit process to save time or money
  • The name on their business card does not match any license you can find
  • They pressure you to decide immediately

If you have concerns about a contractor, you can also review the hiring checklist for a structured approach to vetting contractors before any money changes hands.

What should you do if something goes wrong?

If you believe you have hired an unlicensed contractor or a licensed contractor who has violated their obligations, document everything: contracts, payments, photos of the work, and any communications. You can file a formal complaint with the DBPR, which investigates both unlicensed activity and licensed contractor misconduct.

Florida Contractor Registry provides a starting point for consumers at its complaint guidance page. This site is not a government agency and cannot take enforcement action, but the complaint resource explains how to route concerns to the appropriate authority.

For legal remedies — particularly if you have suffered financial loss — consult a Florida-licensed attorney. This post is educational information, not legal advice.

Common questions

What happens if I hire an unlicensed contractor in Florida?
Work done by an unlicensed contractor may not pass inspection, and you could be held responsible for correcting it at your own expense. Your homeowner's insurance may also decline claims related to unpermitted or unlicensed work. Florida law gives homeowners some recourse, but pursuing it takes time and can be costly.
Is a contractor's license the same as a business license?
No. A local business license or occupational license lets a company operate in a jurisdiction but does not certify trade competency. A Florida contractor license issued through the DBPR demonstrates that the individual passed required exams and met financial responsibility standards. Both may be required, but they serve different purposes.
What is the difference between a state-certified and a state-registered contractor in Florida?
A state-certified contractor holds a license valid throughout Florida without needing additional local approval. A state-registered contractor has passed local jurisdiction requirements but is limited to working in those specific counties or municipalities. Both types appear in the DBPR's public license database.
Can a contractor legally pull permits without a license?
In Florida, pulling a building permit generally requires a licensed contractor for most trade categories. An unlicensed person who pulls permits in someone else's name, or skips permits entirely, is violating state law. Permit records are public and can be checked through your local building department.
Does a subcontractor need to be licensed separately?
Yes. In Florida, subcontractors performing specialty work such as electrical, plumbing, or roofing are generally required to hold their own licenses for those trades. The general contractor's license does not automatically cover all subcontractor activity. You can look up each subcontractor's license status independently.
How do I file a complaint against an unlicensed contractor in Florida?
You can file a complaint with the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation, which investigates unlicensed activity. Florida Contractor Registry also provides a starting point for consumers at its complaint guidance page. Consulting an attorney is advisable if you have suffered financial harm.
Does Florida Contractor Registry replace the official DBPR verification?
No. Florida Contractor Registry is a private directory built from public DBPR records and is not affiliated with the State of Florida. For official, authoritative license verification, use the DBPR's own online portal. This registry is a convenient research tool, not a substitute for the state's official records.
